About

Mechanics, actuation, and control—designed together.

I am an M.S. student in Mechanical Engineering at Yonsei University, advised by Prof. Jun Young Yoon in the Research of Manufacturing & Mechatronics Laboratory. I am interested in precision systems where mechanical architecture, electromagnetic actuation, and feedback control must be developed as one integrated system.

My current work focuses on an AlNiCo-based active stiffness-tunable XY stage that changes structural resonance to support energy-efficient, large-area scanning. I also work on multi-DOF electromagnetic actuators and have studied force-ripple mechanisms in permanent-magnet linear synchronous motors.

Before graduate study, I completed my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering at Yonsei University, graduating in the top 2% of the department. I am preparing to pursue Ph.D. research beginning in Fall 2027.

AlNiCo-Based Active Stiffness-Tunable XY Stage

Active stiffness tuning for resonance-matched, high-gain control of ultra-low-power, large-area scanning motion.

Role
Personal researcher
My contribution
Proposed the active stiffness-tunable stage concept and implemented stiffness tuning using AlNiCo-based tunable magnets.
Result
Achieved up to a 32.43× reduction in energy consumption compared with a non-stiffness-tunable stage.
Output
Journal manuscript in preparation; ASPE 2026 submission; PRESM 2025 and KSPE 2026 posters.

Force–Current Modeling and Force-Ripple Analysis of an Iron-Core PMLSM

Physics-based analysis of force production and periodic force ripple in an iron-core permanent-magnet linear synchronous motor.

Role
Personal researcher · B.S. thesis
My contribution
Derived a force–current model including d–q axis coupling caused by end and slotting effects.
Method
Applied the Magnetic Field Decomposition Method to the Maxwell stress tensor and validated ripple characteristics using FEMM.
Impact
Established a modeling basis for designing compensation currents that reduce periodic force ripple.

Monolithic Hybrid Reluctance Actuator for In-Plane 3-DOF Motion

Compact single-body electromagnetic actuation for long-stroke in-plane motion and high force/torque capability.

Role
Co-researcher
My contribution
Contributed to the design of a compact 3-DOF (x, y, θz) hybrid reluctance actuator and its flexure mechanism.
Design goal
Maintain in-plane stability and out-of-plane constraint without sacrificing volumetric efficiency.
Result
Demonstrated rapid full-range motion control and high maximum force in a compact monolithic form factor.
